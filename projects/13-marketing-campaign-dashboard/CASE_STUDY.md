# Case study: is your marketing budget going where it works?

**Audience:** a marketing manager or business owner — not an analyst.
Method and full results are in [README.md](README.md).

**Status:** portfolio demonstration on **simulated** data for a
fictional Irish home-goods retailer (six channels, 14 campaigns,
January–October 2026). No real customer or ad-account data was used.

---

## The business problem

Google, Meta, display networks and affiliates each report how many
sales they "drove". Added together, they claim far more sales than you
made. Budget decisions based on those reports tend to reward the
channels that show ads to people who were already about to buy.

## What this demo shows

- A **campaign dashboard** with spend, clicks, click-through rate, cost
  per acquisition and return on ad spend by channel, audience and
  campaign.
- **The over-claim, measured:** the platforms claimed 47,545 sales; only
  26,515 orders had any ad contact, and only 9,513 were actually caused
  by marketing.
- **Which channels really pay back.** In this example, voucher
  affiliates, display retargeting and brand search looked excellent on
  "last click" reports but returned between €0.09 and €3.44 per euro
  when measured properly. Generic search and paid social were close to
  what the reports said.
- **The fix is a test, not a fancier model.** Holding back 10% of people
  from one campaign measured its real effect (about 1,700 extra sales,
  with a range of 1,200–2,200) and the range contained the true answer.
  The sophisticated attribution models were no better than simple ones.

## What this could be worth

Retargeting and affiliates together cost €87,000 in this example and
caused very few extra sales. Testing before cutting is essential, but a
business in this position could free up a large part of that budget for
channels that work.

## What we would do next with you

1. Build the dashboard from your ad-platform and shop exports.
2. Reconcile platform conversions with your actual orders.
3. Design a hold-out test on your largest budget line.
4. Re-allocate on evidence, then test the next channel.

## Limits to keep in mind

The "true" effects here were set by the simulation, so the exact
numbers say nothing about your channels. What carries over is the
method: compare reports with real orders, and prove impact with tests.

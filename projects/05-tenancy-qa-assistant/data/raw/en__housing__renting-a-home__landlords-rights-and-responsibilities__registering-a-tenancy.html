Registering a tenancy

Introduction

Landlords must:

  • Register residential tenancies with the Residential Tenancies Board (RTB) every year
  • Update the RTB if information about a tenancy changes

The RTB uses this information to keep a public register of tenancies. This register is a list of all private, approved housing body and cost rental tenancies currently registered with the RTB.

There is a separate register for student specific accommodation tenancies.

The register shows the address of the property. It does not show the identity of the landlord or the tenants.

What tenancies must a landlord register?

Most privately rented properties must be registered with the RTB.

This applies to:

  • Private landlords
  • Approved housing bodies (AHBs)
  • Landlords of student-specific accommodation
  • Landlords of cost rental homes

What tenancies does a landlord not need to register?

These rented properties do not need to be registered with the RTB:

  • Business lettings – properties used for business purposes
  • Holiday lettings – short-term lettings for holidays
  • Former rent-controlled properties or long-occupation lease tenancies – separate legislation applies to these tenancies and they are not covered by residential tenancies legislation
  • Owner-occupied accommodation – for example, if you rent a room in your landlord's home
  • Informal family arrangements – properties where the spouse, parent or child of the landlord lives and there is no formal tenancy agreement in writing. However, the legislation does cover formal agreements between family members, for example, where a lease has been signed.
  • Social housing – housing provided by local authorities
  • Temporary accommodation for hosting refugees from Ukraine with the Accommodation Recognition Payment (ARP)

Do I need to register a home I rent out in my back garden?

If you rent out a home in your garden you may need to register the tenancy with the RTB. Some of these tenancies must be registered and others do not. The rules depend on when the home was added and if you got a planning exemption for it.

If the home was in place before 27 July 2026, it must be registered with the RTB if it is rented out. This applies even if the home is first rented out after 27 July 2026. 

The landlord and tenant are covered by residential tenancies legislation and have certain rights and responsibilities.

If the home was added on or after 27 July 2026 and got a planning exemption, it does not have to be registered with the RTB. To get this planning exemption the garden home must meet specific requirements.

In these situations, the person renting the garden home is not a tenant but a licensee. They are in the rented property with the landlord’s consent. This means they have fewer legal protections than a tenant as they are not covered by residential tenancies legislation.

If the home was added from 27 July 2026 and did not get a valid planning exemption, it must be registered with the RTB.

You can find more information about this exemption in our page ‘Planning permission for altering your home’ and in the specific regulations. You can contact your local authority if you have any questions.

How often must tenancies be registered?

Landlords are responsible for registering tenancies.

Landlords need to register tenancies each year.

New tenancies must be registered by the landlord within a month of the start of the tenancy.

After that, landlords must re-register the tenancy every year that the same tenancy continues. This must be done within a month of the date of when the tenancy first began.

For example, if a new tenancy began on 4 July 2025, the landlord must register the tenancy by 3 August 2025 (within one month of the tenancy start date). If the tenancy continues for the next 12 months, the landlord must register the tenancy again by 3 August 2026, and then every year by 3 August while the tenancy continues.

The RTB will remind landlords when their tenancy is due for annual registration, if they have up-to-date contact information for the landlord.

The Residential Tenancies (Amendment) Act 2019 sets out the requirement for tenancies to be registered annually.

What information do I need to register a tenancy?

Private landlords and approved housing bodies must give this information when registering a tenancy:

Property information for the rented home

  • The address and Eircode
  • Property type, for example, is it a house or apartment
  • The floor area
  • Number of bedrooms
  • The Building Energy Rating (BER), if applicable
  • The local authority area the property is in

Landlord’s information

  • The landlord’s name, address, date of birth and Personal Public Service number (PPS number)
  • Company name and Companies Registration Office (CRO) number, if the landlord is a company
  • The approved housing body (AHB) number, if the landlord is an AHB

Tenancy information

  • The date the tenancy started
  • The rent and how often it is paid
  • Details about the deposit
  • Any charges that the tenants pay for, such as electricity or gas
  • The term of the lease if it is a fixed-term lease
  • If it is a sub-letting
  • Details of any management company or authorised agent

Tenant information

  • Number of tenants and if they are over or under 18
  • Name, date of birth and contact details for all tenants aged 18 and over

The RTB advises you to include the PPS number and date of birth for your tenants, if possible. This is not a mandatory requirement, but it can help if there is a dispute.

Telling the RTB about changes to rent

If you change the rent, you must tell the RTB.

You must send the completed RTB Notice of Rent Review form (pdf) to your tenant at least 90 days before the new rent begins.

Since 14 September 2026, you will have 7 days from when the tenant is served the notice of rent review to send a copy to the RTB. Previously you had to send it to the tenant and the RTB at the same time.

If you do not follow these rules, the notice is invalid.

You also need to update the RTB tenancy registration record with details of the new rent within a month of the rent changing.

Read more about this in our page on rent increases.

How do I know if my tenancy is registered?

You can view the public register to check that your tenancy has been registered.

If you are in student-specific accommodation, you can check the separate register for student specific accommodation tenancies.

The RTB can take action against landlords who do not register tenancies. See 'Penalties for not registering a tenancy with the RTB' below for more information.

Even if your landlord hasn’t registered your tenancy, you can still use the RTB’s dispute resolution service. Landlords can only use this service if they have registered the tenancy.

How much does it cost to register?

The fees for registering a tenancy with the RTB are different depending on the type of tenancy. For example, the fee to register an AHB tenancy is different to the fee to register a private rented tenancy.

There are certain exemptions from annual registration fees, see below.

Registration fees for private, student-specific and cost rental tenancies

The basic fee for registering a private rented, student-specific or cost rental tenancy with the RTB is €40 a year.

This applies if the RTB gets the completed application within a month of the start of the tenancy. There is a late fee of €10 per month for each month that the registration is late.

If you are registering up to 10 tenancies in the same building at the same time, you can pay a combined fee of €170, if you register on time.

You do not have to pay an extra fee for any tenancy changes or further registrations you make within 12 months of registering.

Registration fees for approved housing bodies (AHBs)

The standard fee for registering an AHB tenancy is €20 a year. This rate applies if the tenancy is registered within a month of the start of the tenancy.

There is a late fee of €5 per month for each month that the registration is late.

If you are registering up to 10 AHB tenancies in the same building you can pay a combined fee of €85, if you register them on time.

You do not have to pay an extra fee for any tenancy changes or further registrations you make within 12 months of registering.

Late fees

If you are late registering a tenancy, late fees apply for each month that the registration is late.

Exemptions from annual registration fees

Before annual registration, tenancies were registered each time a new tenancy began. For example, when new tenants moved in under a new lease. An ongoing tenancy was also re-registered after 6 years. This is known as a ‘Further Part 4’ tenancy.

There is a temporary exemption from annual registration fees for landlords who have ‘Further Part 4’ tenancies. This means they do not have to pay annual registration fees for the time remaining on their ‘Further Part 4’ tenancy. So, if there are 3 years left on a ‘Further Part 4’ tenancy, the landlord does not have to pay annual registration fees for those 3 years. The tenancy must still be registered annually.

This temporary annual registration fee waiver is set out in the Residential Tenancies (Amendment) Act 2021.

For more information on annual registration and the temporary fee exemption, see the RTB’s website.

Information sharing

The RTB can share information with local authorities, who enforce the regulations on minimum standards and rent books.

It can also share information with the Department of Social Protection and the Revenue Commissioners.

How to register a tenancy with the RTB

You can register a tenancy online or by post or email.

You can register a tenancy:

  • Online using the RTB’s Tenancy Registration Account. You need to set up a registration account to do this, if you do not already have one. The RTB’s website has information about how to do this. If you need to create an online account for an approved housing body (AHB) you should contact AHB@rtb.ie.
  • By post or email by downloading the RTB 1 Form (pdf). Complete the form and send it back to the RTB. You can return it by post to the RTB’s address below or email it to registrations@rtb.ie. You should keep a copy of the form and proof of postage.

There is a different process for registering tenancies in student-specific accommodation. See the RTB’s website for information on how to do this.

If you have questions about the registration process, you can email registrations@rtb.ie.

When a tenancy has been registered and the fees paid the RTB will send letters to the landlord and tenants confirming that the tenancy has been registered.

Penalties for not registering a tenancy with the RTB

Landlords who do not register a tenancy can be dealt with in a number of ways.

Criminal prosecution

Landlords can be criminally prosecuted, which can result in:

  • A criminal conviction
  • A fine of up to €5,000
  • Imprisonment for up to 12 months
  • An additional penalty of €1,000 for each day the tenancy is not registered
  • Having to pay the RTB's legal costs

Investigation and sanction

Alternatively, the RTB can investigate and sanction the landlord. This can result in:

  • A sanction of up to €15,000
  • €15,000 in costs for each unregistered tenancy

Fixed payment fines

Since 14 September 2026, the RTB can issue landlords with a fixed payment notice if they break certain rental laws. A landlord can be fined €100 for not registering a tenancy.

A landlord has 28 days from the date of the notice to pay the fine. If the landlord does not to pay the fine, the RTB can investigate or prosecute the landlord,

Read more about these penalties on the RTB’s website.
